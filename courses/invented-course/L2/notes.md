# Lesson 2 — How Glimmerworts Get Energy

Glimmerworts get their energy from heat. Warm vents in the cavern floor release tiny heat-carrying grains called calorite.

Inside glimmerwort cells are organelles called thermosomes. A thermosome absorbs calorite and uses its heat to build an energy molecule called glim. One calorite grain yields 5 glim.

Glim is the glimmerwort's energy currency. Its most important use is to power the lumeplasts in the crown, which turn glim into light.

## Cold periods

Thermosomes only work at temperatures of 12 °C or warmer. Below 12 °C they go dormant and stop making glim. During cold periods the glimmerwort switches to slow-burn: it breaks down an orange fat it has stored, called amberlipid. Slow-burn produces only 1 glim per grain of amberlipid, so a glimmerwort in slow-burn has much less energy and its light grows dim.

## Why glow?

The light from the crown attracts drift-moths. When drift-moths land on the crown, selite dust falls from their wings onto the glimmerwort and drifts down to the root-mat, where it is absorbed. A brighter glimmerwort attracts more drift-moths and therefore gets more selite.
