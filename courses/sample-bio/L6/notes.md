# Lesson 6 — Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis is the process plants, algae and some bacteria use to capture energy from sunlight and store it in sugar. In plants and algae it happens in the chloroplasts. The overall equation is:

6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂

In words: carbon dioxide and water go in, and with the energy of light they are turned into glucose and oxygen.

## Inside the chloroplast

A chloroplast has an outer and an inner membrane. Inside are stacks of flattened membrane sacs called thylakoids. A stack of thylakoids is called a granum (plural: grana). The thick fluid surrounding the thylakoids is called the stroma.

## Pigments and color

The thylakoid membranes contain chlorophyll, the main pigment of photosynthesis. Chlorophyll absorbs mostly red and blue light and reflects green light, which is why leaves look green. The light energy absorbed by chlorophyll is what powers photosynthesis.

## Stage 1: The light-dependent reactions

The light-dependent reactions take place in the thylakoid membranes. Light energy absorbed by chlorophyll excites electrons, raising them to a higher energy level. To replace these electrons, water molecules are split into oxygen, hydrogen ions and electrons. The oxygen released by photosynthesis comes from these split water molecules, not from carbon dioxide. The oxygen leaves the leaf as a waste product.

The energy of the excited electrons is used to make two energy-carrying molecules: ATP and NADPH. These carry energy to the second stage.

## Stage 2: The Calvin cycle

The Calvin cycle takes place in the stroma. It does not use light directly, so it is also called the light-independent reactions. In the Calvin cycle, carbon dioxide from the air is attached to an existing organic molecule, a step called carbon fixation. The enzyme that fixes the carbon dioxide is called rubisco. Using the energy of ATP and the electrons from NADPH, the cycle turns the fixed carbon into a three-carbon sugar, which the plant uses to build glucose and other molecules.

## What affects the rate of photosynthesis

- **Light intensity**: more light increases the rate, until the rate levels off because another factor becomes limiting.
- **Carbon dioxide concentration**: more CO₂ increases the rate, until it levels off.
- **Temperature**: the rate increases with temperature up to an optimum; at higher temperatures the enzymes of the Calvin cycle stop working well and the rate drops.

## Photosynthesis and cellular respiration

The two processes are linked. The products of photosynthesis, glucose and oxygen, are the reactants of cellular respiration. The products of cellular respiration, carbon dioxide and water, are the reactants of photosynthesis. Plants do both: they make glucose by photosynthesis in their chloroplasts and break it down by cellular respiration in their mitochondria, day and night.
