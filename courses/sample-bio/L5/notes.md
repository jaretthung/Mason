# Lesson 5 — Cellular Respiration

Cellular respiration is the process cells use to release the energy stored in glucose and capture it in ATP. In eukaryotic cells most of it happens in the mitochondria. The overall equation is:

C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂ → 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + energy (ATP)

In words: glucose and oxygen go in; carbon dioxide, water and ATP come out. The carbon dioxide you breathe out is made by cellular respiration.

Cellular respiration happens in three stages.

## Stage 1: Glycolysis

Glycolysis takes place in the cytoplasm, outside the mitochondria. In glycolysis, one molecule of glucose (6 carbons) is split into two molecules of pyruvate (3 carbons each). Glycolysis does not need oxygen. It produces a net gain of 2 ATP and also 2 NADH. NADH is an electron carrier: it carries high-energy electrons to the last stage.

## Stage 2: The Krebs cycle

If oxygen is available, the pyruvate moves into the mitochondrion. In the matrix, the fluid inside the inner membrane, each pyruvate is converted to a molecule called acetyl-CoA, which enters the Krebs cycle (also called the citric acid cycle). The Krebs cycle breaks the remaining carbon down and releases it as carbon dioxide. For each glucose, the Krebs cycle makes 2 ATP and loads many electron carriers: NADH and FADH₂.

## Stage 3: The electron transport chain

The electron transport chain is a series of proteins in the inner mitochondrial membrane, the folded cristae. NADH and FADH₂ drop off their electrons there. (See the video for how this stage works and the role of oxygen.)

## How much ATP?

Altogether, cellular respiration produces about 30 to 32 ATP from one molecule of glucose. Most of this ATP is made in the electron transport chain.

## Without oxygen: fermentation

When there is no oxygen, the electron transport chain stops, and the Krebs cycle stops too. Cells can still make a small amount of ATP through glycolysis, but glycolysis needs a supply of a molecule called NAD⁺. Fermentation is a set of reactions that turns NADH back into NAD⁺ so glycolysis can keep going. Fermentation itself makes no extra ATP, so a cell relying on glycolysis plus fermentation gets only 2 ATP per glucose.

There are two common types:

- **Lactic acid fermentation**: pyruvate is turned into lactate (lactic acid). Your muscle cells do this during hard exercise when they cannot get oxygen fast enough.
- **Alcoholic fermentation**: pyruvate is turned into ethanol and carbon dioxide. Yeast does this. The carbon dioxide makes bread dough rise, and the ethanol is the alcohol in beer and wine.

Respiration that uses oxygen is called aerobic. Processes that do not use oxygen, such as glycolysis and fermentation, are called anaerobic.
