# Lesson 2 — Organelles: The Parts of a Eukaryotic Cell

A eukaryotic cell works like a small factory. Each organelle has its own job.

## Nucleus

The nucleus is the cell's control center. It holds the cell's DNA, which carries the instructions for making proteins. The nucleus is surrounded by the nuclear envelope, a double membrane with openings called nuclear pores that let materials move in and out. Inside the nucleus, DNA is loosely packed as a material called chromatin. A dark region inside the nucleus, the nucleolus, makes the parts used to build ribosomes.

## Ribosomes

Ribosomes are the organelles that make proteins. They are not surrounded by a membrane. Some ribosomes float free in the cytoplasm and make proteins that are used inside the cell. Other ribosomes are attached to the endoplasmic reticulum.

## Endoplasmic reticulum

The endoplasmic reticulum (ER) is a network of folded membranes. Rough ER is covered with ribosomes, which gives it a bumpy look under the microscope; it makes proteins that will be sent out of the cell or placed in membranes. Smooth ER has no ribosomes; it makes lipids (fats) and helps break down drugs and poisons, which is why liver cells have a lot of smooth ER.

## Golgi apparatus

The Golgi apparatus is a stack of flattened membrane sacs. It modifies, sorts and packages proteins and lipids it receives from the ER, then ships them in small membrane bubbles called vesicles to where they are needed. It works like the cell's post office.

The path of a protein that will be released from the cell is: ribosome on the rough ER → inside the ER → vesicle → Golgi apparatus → vesicle → plasma membrane → released outside the cell.

## Lysosomes

Lysosomes are small sacs filled with digestive enzymes. They break down worn-out organelles, food particles and bacteria that the cell has taken in. Lysosomes are common in animal cells.

## Vacuoles

Vacuoles are membrane sacs that store water, food and wastes. Plant cells have one large central vacuole that can take up most of the cell. When the central vacuole is full of water, it pushes outward against the cell wall, which keeps the plant cell firm. This outward push is called turgor pressure. A plant wilts when its vacuoles lose water.

## Mitochondria

Mitochondria are the site of cellular respiration, the process that releases energy from food and stores it in ATP, the cell's energy-carrying molecule. A mitochondrion is surrounded by two membranes. The inner membrane is folded into many layers called cristae, which give it a large surface area. Cells that use a lot of energy, such as muscle cells, have many mitochondria.

## Chloroplasts

Chloroplasts are found in plant cells and algae. They are the site of photosynthesis, the process that captures energy from sunlight and uses it to make sugar. Chloroplasts contain the green pigment chlorophyll. Like mitochondria, chloroplasts are surrounded by two membranes.

## Cell wall

The cell wall is a stiff layer outside the plasma membrane that supports and protects the cell. Plant cell walls are made of cellulose. Fungi also have cell walls, but theirs are made of chitin. Animal cells have no cell wall.

## Cytoskeleton

The cytoskeleton is a network of protein fibers throughout the cytoplasm. It gives the cell its shape, holds organelles in place and helps the cell and its parts move.

## Plant cells and animal cells

Plant cells have three structures that animal cells lack: a cell wall, chloroplasts and a large central vacuole. Both plant and animal cells have mitochondria.

## Where mitochondria and chloroplasts came from

The endosymbiotic theory states that mitochondria and chloroplasts were once free-living prokaryotes that were swallowed by a larger cell and began living inside it. The evidence: both organelles have their own DNA, both have their own ribosomes, both are surrounded by two membranes, and both divide by splitting in two, the way bacteria do.
